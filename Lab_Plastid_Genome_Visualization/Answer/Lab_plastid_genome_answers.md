# Part E. Questions to Answer

### 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?
- The selected plant is *Plumeria rubra* cultivar *Acutifolia*, a member of the family Apocynaceae. Its complete chloroplast genome is deposited in NCBI GenBank under accession MN812495.1.

### 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?
- The complete plastid genome is 153,912 base pairs (bp) in length.

### 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.
- Yes. The OGDRAW map shows the typical quadripartite organization of the chloroplast genome.

* **LSC (Large Single-Copy):** the largest region of the genome, shown across the upper portion of the circular map. It is 84,852 bp long.
* **SSC (Small Single-Copy):** the smaller single-copy region, shown at the lower portion of the map. It is 18,036 bp long.
* **IRa (Inverted Repeat A):** one of the two repeated regions, located between the LSC and SSC regions on the right side of the map. It is 25,512 bp long.
* **IRb (Inverted Repeat B):** the second inverted repeat, located opposite IRa on the left side of the map. It is also 25,512 bp long.

Together, these regions form the characteristic LSC–IR–SSC–IR quadripartite structure of the plastid genome.

### 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are:
* **rbcL** – encodes the large subunit of Rubisco and participates in carbon fixation.
* **matK** – encodes a maturase involved in RNA processing.
* **atpA** – encodes a subunit of ATP synthase involved in ATP production.
- These genes are among the annotated protein-coding genes associated with the plastid's photosynthesis, energy-production, and RNA-processing functions.

### 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.
- One example is ndhF, which is associated with the NADH dehydrogenase complex and participates in electron transport and photosynthetic processes.

### 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?
- An example is rpl2, which is located within the inverted-repeat region. Because the chloroplast genome contains two homologous IR regions, genes located within these regions can occur in two copies, with one copy associated with each IR. Thus, the duplicated appearance of an IR-associated gene is a consequence of the two IR regions, rather than two unrelated copies of the gene.

### 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.
- One photosynthesis-related gene shown on the map is psbA. It encodes a core protein component of Photosystem II, which participates in the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis. Another important photosynthesis-related gene is rbcL, which encodes the large subunit of Rubisco and contributes to carbon fixation.

### 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?
- The direction of the gene arrows indicates the orientation of the genes and the direction in which they are transcribed. Genes pointing in opposite directions are located on opposite DNA strands. Therefore, the map provides a visual representation of the orientation of genes and their transcriptional direction around the circular plastid genome.

### 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.
- The GC content is not completely uniform across the genome. Some regions show slightly higher or lower GC content than other regions. The overall GC content is about 37.94%.

### 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?
- A graphical map makes the genome easier to understand and study. It shows the locations of genes, the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions, and gene directions in one figure. A raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations is more difficult to visualize and understand.
